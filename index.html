          About this number

          Dose equivalent from galactic cosmic rays, measured on the surface by Curiosity's RAD instrument in Gale Crater and used here as a stand-in for Jezero. It is the same everywhere on this map to within a few percent, so there is no radiation map layer. A solar storm would add to it. A suit gives almost no shielding.

            What this is

            A planner for a walk on Mars. It stacks data from NASA missions over Jezero Crater, finds a safe walking route between science stops, and checks the plan against daylight, terrain, weather and what the suit can carry.

            Data

            Map tiles: NASA Mars Trek (MRO HiRISE and CTX, MGS MOLA, Mars Odyssey THEMIS, Viking). Terrain model: MRO CTX stereo DTM, USGS Astrogeology. Rover track and weather: NASA/JPL-Caltech Mars 2020 (MEDA). Dust record: Montabone et al. climatology from MGS TES, Mars Odyssey THEMIS and MRO MCS. Surface dust cover: MGS TES. Walking speeds: NASA JSC traverse model and EVA Walkback Test. Atmosphere: NASA Mars Fact Sheet. Radiation: MSL Curiosity RAD (Hassler et al.). Consumable rates: NASA EVA Walkback Test. Thermal inertia: Mars Odyssey THEMIS (USGS). Fine terrain: MRO HiRISE DTM (USGS). Wind: Mars 2020 MEDA wind sensor, sols 36 to 65 (NASA PDS Atmospheres Node). Mineral maps: MRO CRISM (NASA PDS Geosciences Node). Sample sites: NASA Mars Rock Samples list. Place names: IAU Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature (USGS). Rock units and sand: USGS SIM 3464. Mars time: Allison & McEwen (2000), as used in NASA GISS Mars24.

            What is assumed

            The safety limits, the dust and temperature thresholds, the habitat's location and the reserve levels for consumables are the team's assumptions, not NASA figures. Walking speeds and consumable rates are from Moon-gravity tests. The wind animation spreads one measured value over the whole map.
